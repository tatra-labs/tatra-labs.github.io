The treemap shows one snapshot: how many people hold each job today. This page asks the three questions a snapshot cannot answer. Where the jobs are and how that has shifted over seven years (BLS OEWS, 2018–2024). How far remote work spread, by state and by occupation, and whether employers and job seekers moved together (Census ACS 2018–2024, Indeed Hiring Lab 2019–2026). And, for remote software jobs specifically, which technologies and roles are rising or falling (15 years of Ask HN: Who is hiring?). It is a tool for someone deciding what to learn and where to apply — not a report or a forecast.

Every number on screen comes from a file in data/ of this repository, fetched and parsed by the scripts in pipeline/. Nothing is smoothed, back-cast or interpolated to make a line look tidier. Where a series has a hole, the hole is drawn. The address bar carries whatever you have selected on each tab, so a particular occupation, state, year or set of technologies can be reloaded or sent to someone. Two of these datasets do not mean what their names suggest, and one of them is not a sample of the US labour market at all — read the caveats before you conclude anything.

Eight things this data cannot tell you (worth two minutes before you draw a conclusion)
  • The ACS has no 2020. The Census Bureau never released a standard 1-year file for 2020; pandemic data collection failed. Every 2020 point in the work-from-home series is missing and is drawn as a gap, never a straight line. The step from 2019 to 2021 spans two survey years, not one.
  • OEWS occupation codes break across SOC vintages, so a gap is not a decline. Software Developers is 15-1132 in 2018 (SOC 2010), then a combined 15-1256 in 2019–2020, then 15-1252 from 2021. A naive seven-year line for 15-1252 shows nothing before 2021 — that is a renumbering, not an industry collapse. Each series carries the exact years its code was published, and discontinuous series are labelled as such.
  • A # wage is a censored high value, not a missing one. OEWS prints # when the median is at or above the top code — $208,000/yr for 2018–2021, $239,200/yr for 2022 onward. It means “at least this much”. ** (estimate not released) and * (wage not available) are genuinely missing. They are drawn differently here, because treating top-coded pay as missing biases high-paying occupations downward.
  • May 2020 OEWS is a COVID-affected reference period. National employment falls 5.3% from 2019 and California drops roughly 950,000 jobs. Do not read 2019 → 2020 → 2021 as a structural shift.
  • Indeed’s “remote” is not the ACS’s “worked from home”. Indeed measures the share of job postings whose text mentions remote or hybrid work — a demand-side signal that lumps hybrid in with fully remote. The ACS measures workers who did not commute in the reference week — a supply-side, primary-mode, place-of-residence measure that undercounts hybrid arrangements. They answer different questions and are never plotted as one quantity. Indeed’s job-seeker search series is a third thing again: what workers typed, not what employers offered.
  • Each Indeed series is drawn only to its own last month. The brief for this page assumed the per-sector remote series was discontinued at 2023-05-26. It is not: in this retrieval every country–sector series runs to 2026-07, which the repository’s own dataset notes confirm. Rather than hard-code either date, each series carries the last month it actually has data for, and no line is drawn past it. Indeed also changed its seasonal-adjustment method in November 2024 and revised history, so figures here will not match older Hiring Lab articles.
  • Ask HN: Who is hiring? is a biased convenience sample. It is YC-adjacent startups posting to one monthly thread — not the US labour market, not weighted, not random. Thread volume fell from 10,259 postings in 2021 to 2,644 in 2026 (a partial year), so counts are not a hiring indicator; only shares of postings are informative, and even those describe this thread’s crowd. 2026 is partial everywhere and is marked as such. Significance intervals shown here are a noise floor, not a claim about the labour market.
  • Some geographies and estimates are not what a total suggests. OEWS publishes 54 state-equivalents but the map has 52 shapes: Guam and the US Virgin Islands have no polygon, so summing the map does not reproduce the national figure. Puerto Rico must be excluded from any “sum the states” ACS total. Metro location quotients on this page are derived from employment shares, not published by BLS. And state and national OEWS totals differ slightly because BLS rounds every estimate independently.
