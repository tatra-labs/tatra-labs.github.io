**Chen, Kingma, Salimans, Duan, Dhariwal, Schulman, Sutskever, Abbeel · 2017 · [arXiv:1611.02731](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02731)**

Every other section here asks where information is stored; this one asks what a store may hold. By 2016 several groups had bolted an autoregressive RNN decoder onto a VAE and found the latent code unused. Bowman et al. documented it for sentences: the KL term falls to zero, `q(z|x)` collapses onto the prior, and the model becomes an unconditional language model with a decorative encoder. The accepted explanation was optimisation failure: early on the posterior carries little information about `x`, matching the prior is cheap, and gradient descent never climbs back out. The fixes were KL annealing and decoder dropout.

This paper answers that a perfect optimiser would do the same, reading the lower bound as a code length. A VAE is a two-part code: `z` under `p(z)`, then the residual under `p(x|z)`. Bits-back coding, Hinton and Van Camp's argument from chapter 1, notes that the sender's choice of `z ~ q(z|x)` carries a secondary message the receiver recovers by rerunning the encoder, making the true expected length the negative lower bound. Rearranged, that is `E[−log p(x)] + KL(q(z|x)||p(z|x))`: at least `H(data)`, plus a surcharge on every nat routed through an imprecise posterior. No posterior family closes that gap. If the decoder can model the data without `z`, the true posterior is the prior, `q` matches it, and the surcharge disappears. Collapse is the optimum, not a failure to reach it: what the decoder models locally stays local, and only the remainder reaches the code.

The exploit: replace the full autoregressive factorisation with `p(x_i | z, x_WindowAround(i))`, a PixelCNN whose receptive field is a small patch above and left of pixel `i`. What that window predicts stays out of the latent; long-range dependency travels through `z`. On statically binarised MNIST, with a six-layer masked-convolution decoder and a 64-dimensional code, the latent carried 13.3 nats (19.2 bits), against 37.3 bits for the identical VAE with a factorised decoder. Decompressions keep the digit's global shape; none is exact, and the binary mask usually differs. On CIFAR10, widening the window from 4x2 to 7x4 leaves the code holding progressively rougher shape as the decoder takes over more structure; a grayscale-only window forces colour back in.

A second change swaps the usual IAF posterior for an autoregressive-flow prior: equivalent along the encoder path, with a deeper generator at no training cost. Together they gave 79.03 nats on statically binarised MNIST, 77.36 on Caltech-101 Silhouettes and 2.95 bits/dim on CIFAR10. It earns its place because chapter 1's compression material does load-bearing work here: a 1993 coding argument predicts an architecture's failure and hands over the knob that fixes it.

> The decoder's receptive field is a specification of what the latent code is not permitted to know.

**The caveat:** the argument is asymptotic, as the paper says — the optimum, not the path to it. Training still needed free bits at 0.01 nats per data dimension, plus a softened variant on CIFAR10; the optimisation problem it demoted did not go away. The window is hand-chosen per dataset: on OMNIGLOT, where small patches carry meaning, the authors report that semantics are not preserved in some decompressions. Latent-variable image models later lost density estimation to diffusion; the collapse diagnosis endured.
