**Alex Graves, Greg Wayne, Ivo Danihelka · 2014 · [arXiv:1410.5401](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5401)**

An LSTM keeps everything it knows in one fixed-width hidden state, so a network asked to hold a sequence verbatim must smear it across the vector it uses for everything else. The copy task shows it: present eight-bit random vectors, then a delimiter, then ask for them back. An LSTM trained on lengths randomised between 1 and 20 degrades rapidly past 20: it fitted a function, not a procedure. What was missing was addressable working memory: the item at `i` kept separate from the procedure operating on it.

The NTM couples a controller to an external memory matrix `M_t` of shape `N × M` — 128 locations of 20 values in all reported experiments. The controller, feedforward or LSTM, emits per head the parameters of one access. Reading is a convex combination, `r_t = Σ_i w_t(i) M_t(i)`, `w_t` a normalised weighting over the 128 rows. Writing splits into erase then add, from LSTM's forget and input gates: `M̃_t(i) = M_{t−1}(i)[1 − w_t(i)e_t]`, then `M_t(i) = M̃_t(i) + w_t(i)a_t`. Nothing indexes: every access touches all 128 rows, weighted, which is what makes it differentiable end to end. That soft read over a bank of vectors recurs throughout this chapter.

The weighting is assembled in stages. Content: the head emits a key `k_t`, takes cosine similarity against every row, and softmaxes it sharpened by a strength `β_t`. Location: an interpolation gate blends that against the head's previous weighting, `w_t^g = g_t w_t^c + (1 − g_t) w_{t−1}`; a distribution `s_t` over integer offsets rotates it by circular convolution modulo `N`; a sharpening exponent `γ_t ≥ 1` reconcentrates what the convolution blurred. The copy solution combines both: content-address the start of the buffer, then advance one location per step. The advance is relative, so it is length-independent.

Trained on lengths 1 to 20, the NTM copies test sequences of 30 and 50 with no retraining. At 120 one vector is duplicated and pushes everything after it a step back, which the paper calls subjectively close to a correct copy. On associative recall — three six-bit vectors per item, 2 to 6 items per episode — it reaches near-zero cost in about 30,000 episodes; LSTM does not in a million. The feedforward copy model has 17,162 parameters against 1,352,969 for the LSTM baseline. It earns its place for showing that content matching works as a memory interface, not just an alignment trick, and that a learned algorithm can outrun its training range.

> Make memory addressing differentiable and a network can learn where to put things, not only what to compute.

**What did not survive:** the hand-built location machinery. The interpolation gate, circular shift and sharpening exponent were bolted on to make iteration possible. The DNC replaced them two years later with a learned temporal link matrix and usage-based allocation; Transformers kept content addressing and dropped the write head. The strain shows in the paper: repeat copy trained with length and repeat count both drawn from 1 to 10, and past that range the network keeps duplicating correctly but "is unable to keep count of how many repeats it has completed, and does not predict the end marker correctly". Never published at a venue; the preprint was last revised in December 2014.
