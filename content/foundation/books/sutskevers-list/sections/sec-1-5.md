**Scott Aaronson, Sean M. Carroll, Lauren Ouellette · 2014 · [arXiv:1405.6903](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.6903)**

Three years on, nobody had plotted the curve. Carroll had argued informally for a quantity that rises and falls, and Gell-Mann before him, but every available measure was either maximized by noise — Boltzmann, Shannon and Kolmogorov entropy all rate the mixed cup as the most complex state — or had no usable approximation, like sophistication and logical depth. This paper takes the one that can be computed and runs it.

The recipe is to compress a blurred copy. The state is an `n x n` bit array, `1` for cream and `0` for coffee, top half all `1`. The interacting rule picks one adjacent pair of differing cells per step, horizontally or vertically, and swaps them, so a cell holds at most one particle: the symmetric exclusion process. The non-interacting rule lets each cream particle random-walk independently, any number to a cell. Estimated entropy is the gzip size of the raw bit array. Estimated **apparent complexity** is the gzip size of a coarse-grained copy: replace each cell by the average over the `g x g` square around it, threshold the floats into three buckets — mostly coffee, mixed, mostly cream — and compress that. Blurring deletes the incidental randomness, so the mixed end state collapses back to something short and only tendril-scale structure survives.

Both models produced the rise-and-fall curve. Peak entropy scaled quadratically with `n` (r² = 0.9999), peak complexity linearly (r² = 0.9798, interacting), time-to-peak quadratically (r² = 0.9878, interacting). The authors also proved that in the non-interacting case the coarse-grained image has complexity at most `log2(n) + log2(t) + O(1)`: for a large enough grain, a Chernoff bound keeps every block near its expectation, leaving a row-by-row gradient recomputable from `n` and `t`. That forced a hunt for the artifact producing a bump where theory said none. The culprit was threshold rounding at the cream-coffee border, where small fluctuations flip cells between buckets. The patch used seven thresholds instead of three and snapped each cell to its row's majority when within one threshold of it. The non-interacting bump flattened; the interacting one held, which is the paper's evidence.

What earns its place is the recipe and its named failure mode: complexity as compressed size after a smoothing pass, with the choice of smoothing carrying the whole distinction between structure and noise. The authors concede the choice is theirs, defended only on the grounds that squares of contiguous cells follow the automaton's causal structure.

> Structure is whatever survives blurring, and the compressed size of the blurred state is a cheap way to weigh it.

**What did not survive: the experiment.** A week after posting, Brent Werness showed the interacting automaton is not an example at all — it follows as a corollary of a 2009 result of Liggett on the symmetric exclusion process, and large-deviation bounds control the interacting case as tightly as the control. The surviving bump was likely incomplete removal of the same border artifact. Aaronson's update of 3 June 2014 says so and calls it a bad choice of model system. arXiv still carries only v1, from 27 May 2014, with the claim intact. Compression bounds Kolmogorov complexity from above only, so no run of gzip could have proved complexity high anyway.
