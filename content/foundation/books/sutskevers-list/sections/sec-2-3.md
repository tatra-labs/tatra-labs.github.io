**Wojciech Zaremba, Ilya Sutskever, Oriol Vinyals · 2014 · [arXiv:1409.2329](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2329)**

**Dropout** is the standard trick against overfitting: during training, randomly switch off a fraction of the units on each pass, so the network cannot lean on any one of them and has to spread the work around. By 2014 it was routine for ordinary feedforward networks, and it simply did not work on recurrent ones.

The practical consequence was a ceiling on size. RNNs had to be kept small, because large ones just memorised the training set. The paper's own baseline shows the cost: the best unregularized LSTM they trained on the Penn Treebank was two layers of 200 units, stopped after 13 epochs, at 114.5 test **perplexity** — a score for language models where lower is better, roughly "how many equally-likely options is the model effectively choosing between at each word". Making the network wider did not help; it only overfitted faster. The going explanation, from Bayer et al. (2013), was that recurrence amplifies the noise dropout injects, and their fix was a deterministic approximation rather than real sampling.

**The actual fix is one sentence long, and the diagram in your head is what matters.** A stacked LSTM cell has two incoming vectors. One comes *up* from the layer below at the same timestep, $h_t^{l-1}$. The other comes *sideways* from the same layer at the previous timestep, $h_{t-1}^l$. Apply dropout to the vertical one and leave the horizontal one alone, so the gate inputs come from $T_{2n,4n}(D(h_t^{l-1}), h_{t-1}^l)$. Nothing else changes: same gates, same additive cell update from the last section, same training loop.

**The justification is a counting argument, and it is the part worth remembering.** Trace one signal from the input at step $t-2$ to the prediction at step $t+2$. It climbs up through the layers, runs along the recurrent path for a while, and exits. Only the vertical hops carry a dropout mask, so the signal is "corrupted by the dropout operator exactly $L + 1$ times", where $L$ is the depth of the stack — a count that does not depend on how many timesteps it crossed. Standard dropout masks every sideways hop as well, so corruption piles up with the length of the sequence, and anything the cell tries to hold for thirty steps is noise by the time anyone reads it. Keeping the horizontal path clean buys the regularization without charging for it in memory.

The numbers carried the argument. A 650-unit two-layer LSTM at 50% dropout, unrolled 35 steps, reached 82.7 test perplexity; a 1500-unit version at 65% dropout reached 78.4 — against 114.5 unregularized and 100.0 for the best previous single model in their table. Averaging 38 large regularized LSTMs together gave 68.7. The effect transferred across domains: WMT'14 English–French BLEU moved 25.9 → 29.03 at dropout 0.2, and Icelandic acoustic frame accuracy rose 68.9 → 70.5 on validation while training accuracy *fell* 71.6 → 69.4 — the signature of a regularizer doing its job, fitting the training set less well in exchange for generalising better. Pham et al. (2013) had reached the same recipe independently for handwriting; the contribution here is the demonstration across four domains, and 78.4 became the number later work reported against.

> Dropout is safe on the vertical connections and destructive on the horizontal ones, because only the horizontal ones accumulate masks as the sequence grows.

**What did not survive:** the rule itself. Gal and Ghahramani (2016) showed the diagnosis was right and the prescription too strong. The damage came from drawing a *fresh* random mask at every timestep, not from touching the recurrent path at all. Draw one mask per sequence and reuse it at every step, and dropout can go on the recurrent connections and the embeddings too: their large variational LSTM reached 75.2 test perplexity against 78.4, and 73.4 when averaging over many masks at test time, which is expensive to run. The original paper also concedes more than its abstract suggests. On image captioning, dropout lifted BLEU from 23.5 to 24.3 — while an ensemble of ten unregularized models scored 24.4 without it.
