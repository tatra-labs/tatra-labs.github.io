**Vaswani, Shazeer, Parmar, Uszkoreit, Jones, Gomez, Kaiser & Polosukhin · 2017 · [arXiv:1706.03762](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)**

Attention fixed the fixed-length bottleneck but left the recurrence underneath it. A recurrent encoder computes `h_t` from `h_{t-1}`, so a sequence of length `n` costs `n` sequential steps no matter how wide the GPU is; batching helps across examples and does nothing within one. Convolutional sequence models cut the sequential count but still needed a stack deep enough for distant positions to reach each other, so the number of layers between two tokens grew with the distance between them — linearly in ConvS2S, logarithmically in ByteNet. The constraint was the time axis itself.

The Transformer removes it by turning the previous section's score-normalize-average recipe on a sequence and itself, with a cheaper scoring function. Scaled dot-product attention is `softmax(QK^T / sqrt(d_k)) V`: every position emits a query, every position emits a key and a value, and each output is a weighted average of all values with weights from query-key dot products. No MLP, no recurrence, one matrix multiply. The scaling term has a specific reason. If the components of `q` and `k` are independent with mean 0 and variance 1, their dot product has mean 0 and variance `d_k`. At `d_k = 64` the logits arrive with a standard deviation of 8, which pushes softmax into the region where its gradients are extremely small. Dividing by `sqrt(d_k)` restores unit variance. The paper notes that without it, additive attention outperforms dot-product attention at large `d_k`.

Multi-head attention splits the work instead of widening it. Rather than one attention over `d_model = 512`, the model projects into `h = 8` heads of `d_k = d_v = 64`, attends in each subspace in parallel, concatenates, and projects back, at roughly the cost of one full-width head. A single softmax-weighted average blends everything into one blur; eight let separate heads track separate relations. Since attention treats its input as a set, order has to be injected: sinusoids whose wavelengths form a geometric progression, `PE(pos,2i) = sin(pos/10000^(2i/d_model))` with cosine in the odd dimensions, added to the input embeddings.

The payoff is in Table 1. A self-attention layer needs `O(1)` sequential operations against `O(n)` for a recurrent layer, and the maximum path length between any two positions is `O(1)` against `O(n)`. Cost per layer is `O(n^2·d)` rather than `O(n·d^2)`, the cheaper side whenever `n < d` — true of sentences, not of documents. The base model trained for 100,000 steps in 12 hours on eight P100 GPUs; the big model took 300,000 steps and 3.5 days, for 28.4 BLEU on WMT 2014 English–German and 41.8 on English–French.

> Deleting recurrence moved the sequence dimension out of time and into a matrix multiply, which turned scale into an engineering problem rather than an architectural one.

**What did not survive:** the positional encoding. The authors reported that learned embeddings performed about the same, and chose sinusoids partly on the hypothesis that they would extrapolate to sequence lengths longer than those seen in training — a hypothesis the paper did not test and that later work found weak. Absolute position gave way to relative schemes, then to rotary embeddings and ALiBi. The `O(n^2)` term stayed, and is still the reason long context costs what it does.
