**Dzmitry Bahdanau, Kyunghyun Cho, Yoshua Bengio · 2014 · [arXiv:1409.0473](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0473)**

The encoder–decoder translators of 2014 read a source sentence into one fixed-length vector and generated from it. In the RNNencdec baseline used here, the encoder and decoder each have 1000 hidden units, so a fifty-word sentence and a five-word sentence are compressed into the same 1000 numbers. Cho et al. (2014) had already measured the consequence: BLEU falls off sharply as the input lengthens. The budget for the source does not grow with the source.

The fix, presented at ICLR 2015, has two halves. The encoder becomes a bidirectional RNN of gated units, 1000 per direction, and emits one annotation per source position, `h_j = [h→_j ; h←_j]`. Its output is a variable-length sequence of `T_x` vectors, not a single summary. The decoder then builds its own summary fresh at every output step. Before emitting word `i`, it scores each annotation against its own previous state `s_{i-1}` with a one-hidden-layer MLP of 1000 units, `e_ij = v_aᵀ tanh(W_a s_{i-1} + U_a h_j)`, normalizes those scores across `j` with a softmax to get weights `α_ij`, and takes the weighted sum `c_i = Σ_j α_ij h_j`. The decoder conditions on `c_i` instead of a shared `c`.

What makes this work is that nothing is discrete. Classical alignment treats the source-to-target correspondence as a latent variable inferred in a separate stage. Here the weighted sum is differentiable, so gradients from the translation loss flow back into the scoring MLP, and alignment is learned jointly with translation under one log-likelihood objective. There is no alignment supervision. `U_a h_j` does not depend on `i`, so it is precomputed once per sentence.

On WMT'14 English–French — 348M words after Axelrod data selection, a 30,000-word shortlist per language, tested on news-test-2014 — RNNencdec-50 scores 17.82 BLEU and RNNsearch-50 scores 26.75, or 28.45 when trained to convergence. Moses reaches 33.30 on the full test set. Restricted to sentences with no unknown words the ordering reverses: 36.15 for RNNsearch-50* against 35.63 for Moses, which also had an extra 418M-word monolingual corpus. RNNsearch-30 (21.50) beats RNNencdec-50 (17.82). The length curve flattens; RNNsearch-50 shows no degradation past fifty words. The `α_ij` matrices are legible, and show non-monotonic reordering — *European Economic Area* into *zone économique européenne*. Score, normalize, average: every attention variant since is that recipe with a different scoring function.

> A decoder that recomputes what it reads at every step never has to compress the whole input into one vector.

**What the paper got wrong:** it waved off its own cost. The alignment model "needs to be evaluated `T_x × T_y` times for each sentence pair", which the authors judged tolerable because "most of input and output sentences are only 15–40 words". That product is now the binding constraint on context length and the target of most efficiency work in the field. The recurrent scaffolding around it did not survive either.
