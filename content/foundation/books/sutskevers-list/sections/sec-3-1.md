**Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever, Geoffrey E. Hinton · 2012 · [NeurIPS 25](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/hash/c399862d3b9d6b76c8436e924a68c45b-Abstract.html)**

Before 2012 an ImageNet entry was a pipeline of hand-designed parts: local descriptors such as SIFT or HOG, an encoding step, a linear classifier on top. Each stage was tuned separately and the features never adapted to the task. Convolutional networks had worked on small problems since LeNet-5 in 1998, but nobody had trained one for 1.2 million images and 1000 classes. The best non-network entry in the 2012 contest finished at 26.2% top-5 error.

The paper trains one network end to end — five convolutional layers, three fully connected, a 1000-way softmax, 60 million parameters and 650,000 neurons — and makes four decisions that let it converge. The nonlinearity is `max(0, x)` rather than `tanh`. A saturating unit has near-zero gradient once its input is large in magnitude, so learning stalls; the rectifier's derivative is exactly `1` on the positive side and does not decay as activations grow. A four-layer net reached 25% training error on CIFAR-10 six times faster with rectifiers than with `tanh`. The model is then split across two GTX 580s, because 3GB on one card cannot hold it. Half the kernels sit on each GPU; layers 2, 4 and 5 read only from maps on their own card while layer 3 reads from both, so the cards exchange activations at one point. Against a single-GPU net with half the kernels per layer, the split cut top-1 error 1.7% and top-5 1.2%. Ninety epochs took five to six days.

The other two decisions attack overfitting. Dropout zeroes each neuron in the first two fully connected layers with probability `0.5` on every forward pass, so no unit can rely on a particular partner being present; at test time all units are active with outputs halved to match the expected activation. It roughly doubled the iterations to convergence. Augmentation manufactures training data from labels already held: random `224x224` crops and horizontal reflections out of `256x256` images, a nominal factor of 2048 though the variants are heavily correlated, plus a jitter that shifts RGB values along the principal components of the pixel covariance, worth over 1% top-1 alone. Test predictions average ten patches — four corners, centre, and their reflections.

The ILSVRC-2012 entry finished at 15.3% top-5 test error against 26.2% for the runner-up, an eleven-point margin in a contest normally decided by one or two. Within a year essentially every serious entry was a convolutional net on GPUs. The paper earns its place as the first clean evidence that the binding constraint was arithmetic and labelled data rather than the model class — the argument Sutskever spent the following decade extending.

> Given enough labelled data and enough arithmetic, one generic deep network beats a decade of hand-designed features.

**What did not survive:** local response normalization, a lateral inhibition step dividing each activation by a function of its neighbours across kernels at the same spatial position. The paper credits it with 1.4% top-1 and 1.2% top-5; the VGG authors reported two years later that it gave no ILSVRC gain while costing memory and compute time, and batch normalization replaced the idea outright. The headline 15.3% is also an average over seven networks, two of them pre-trained on the larger 2011 release — a single model scored 18.2% top-5 on validation.
