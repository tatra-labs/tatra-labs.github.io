**Christopher Olah · 2015 · [colah.github.io](https://colah.github.io/posts/2015-08-Understanding-LSTMs/)**

The character RNN of the previous section trains, but not far back. Backpropagating a loss from step `t` to step `t-k` multiplies `k` Jacobians, each carrying a factor of the recurrent weight matrix `W` and a `tanh'` term bounded by 1. The product collapses toward zero or blows up, so a gradient that has to cross a hundred steps arrives as noise or as an overflow; Hochreiter (1991) and Bengio et al. (1994) had shown why. The LSTM (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, 1997) had been the standard answer for eighteen years, but its published descriptions were coupled equations and names — constant error carousel, gate units — that said nothing about what moved where. Plenty of people trained LSTMs out of a library with no mental picture of the cell.

Olah's post, dated 27 August 2015, supplies the picture. The cell state `C_t` runs as a horizontal line across the top of the diagram, touched by exactly one pointwise multiply and one pointwise add — "kind of like a conveyor belt". Everything else in the cell decides what comes off the belt and what goes on. A gate is a sigmoid layer feeding a pointwise multiply: the sigmoid emits a vector in `[0,1]` that scales each component, 0 blocking and 1 passing.

Three gates read the same concatenation `[h_{t-1}, x_t]`. The forget gate `f_t` scales the incoming cell state. The input gate `i_t` scales a candidate vector `C̃_t = tanh(W_C·[h_{t-1}, x_t] + b_C)`. The two combine additively: `C_t = f_t * C_{t-1} + i_t * C̃_t`. The output gate `o_t` sets how much of the state is exposed, giving `h_t = o_t * tanh(C_t)`. Olah runs a language-model example through it: a cell holding the current subject's gender so a later pronoun agrees, cleared when a new subject arrives. The gradient argument falls out of the update's shape. `∂C_t/∂C_{t-1}` is `f_t` elementwise — no weight matrix, no saturating nonlinearity. Where a unit's forget gate sits near 1, error travels back through it across many steps essentially unattenuated.

The post became the reference explanation, and the conveyor belt is now the default drawing of an LSTM. It also surveys variants without picking a winner: peephole connections, coupled forget/input gates, and the GRU (Cho et al., 2014), which merges forget and input into one update gate and folds the cell state into the hidden state. Olah reports Greff et al. (2015) finding the popular variants "all about the same", and Jozefowicz et al. (2015) testing more than ten thousand architectures, some of them better than LSTMs on particular tasks. It earns its place because the gated additive path it makes visible outlived the LSTM itself: the residual stream in a Transformer is the same trick with the gates taken out.

> Keep a channel that is only scaled and added to, never pushed through a weight matrix, and gradients will travel along it.

**The caveat:** the post asserts the gradient fix rather than deriving it, and the conveyor-belt image suggests LSTMs cannot vanish. They can. The cell-state path accumulates the product of forget gates, so a unit whose `f_t` settles below 1 decays error at its own exponential rate. Olah credits the architecture to Hochreiter & Schmidhuber (1997) and leaves refinements to a footnote, so the forget gate reads as original; Gers, Schmidhuber and Cummins added it in 2000. His closing prediction, that attention was the next big step, named the right mechanism and the wrong carrier: attention arrived and then deleted the recurrence the post explains.
