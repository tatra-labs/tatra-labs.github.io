**Kaiming He, Xiangyu Zhang, Shaoqing Ren, Jian Sun · 2016 · [arXiv:1603.05027](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05027)**

The original block was not the clean identity path it is usually drawn as. It computed `x_{l+1} = ReLU(x_l + F(x_l))`: the addition happened, and then a ReLU sat on the result. That ReLU is on the trunk, not the branch, so a signal running from layer 1 to layer 1000 passed through a thousand nonlinearities anyway. At ordinary depths this went unnoticed. At extreme depth it did not, and the CIFAR warmup schedule was a symptom.

This paper writes the block generally as `y_l = h(x_l) + F(x_l)`, `x_{l+1} = f(y_l)`, then asks what happens when both `h` and `f` are the identity. The forward pass telescopes: `x_L = x_l + Σ F(x_i)`, so any deep activation is a shallow one plus a sum of residuals rather than a product of transforms. Differentiating gives `∂E/∂x_l = ∂E/∂x_L · (1 + ∂/∂x_l Σ F)`. The `1` is the point: the shrinking term is added to a constant rather than multiplied into one, so no product of small Jacobians can zero the path out. Making `f` the identity means the nonlinearity has to move, and it moves to the head of the branch: BN → ReLU → conv → BN → ReLU → conv, added onto the shortcut with nothing afterward.

The ablations carry the argument. On ResNet-110/CIFAR-10 the plain identity shortcut gives 6.61% error. A `1x1` convolution on the shortcut gives 12.22% — strictly more expressive than an identity, and it loses by more than five points, which locates the problem in optimization rather than capacity. Scaling both shortcut and branch by 0.5 gives 12.35%; scaling only the shortcut fails to converge, as does dropout on the shortcut; the best exclusive-gating variant reaches 8.70%. Ordering matters the same way: BN after the addition 8.17%, ReLU before the addition 7.84%, ReLU-only pre-activation 6.71%, full pre-activation 6.37%.

With the reordered unit the 1001-layer CIFAR-10 network reaches 4.92% against 7.61% for the same depth built the old way, and CIFAR-100 at that depth goes from 27.82% to 22.71%. On ImageNet at 320×320 crops, ResNet-200 improves from 21.8%/6.0% to 20.7%/5.3% top-1/top-5. Warmup is no longer needed. The rule — keep the residual stream unmodulated, put the normalization inside the branch — is what pre-LN transformers adopted, and it is why the residual stream described above stayed a clean sum rather than accumulating gates.

> Keep the residual stream free of everything but additions, and the gradient path becomes `1 + something` instead of a product.

**What did not survive:** the 1000-layer network. At ResNet-110 the gain over the original block was 0.24 points, so the payoff arrives only at depths nobody deployed; width and better blocks bought more than depth did. The paper also names its own limit — the original ResNet-200 had *lower* training error than ResNet-152 and worse validation error, so part of what pre-activation fixed on ImageNet was overfitting through BN placement, not gradient flow. The ResNet-50 shipped in most vision libraries is still the 2015 post-activation block.
