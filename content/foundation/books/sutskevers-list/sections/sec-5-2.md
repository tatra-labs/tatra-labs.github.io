**Dario Amodei, Sundaram Ananthanarayanan, Rishita Anubhai, Jingliang Bai, Eric Battenberg et al. (Baidu Research) · 2015 (ICML 2016) · [arXiv:1512.02595](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.02595)**

A 2014 speech recognizer was a stack of separately trained parts. An acoustic model scored short slices of audio against phoneme states. A lexicon, written by linguists, mapped each word to its phoneme sequence. An HMM handled the alignment between audio frames and states. An n-gram language model and a decoder combined all the scores at the end.

**No stage in that stack optimized transcription accuracy directly**, which is the flaw worth sitting with — each part was trained on its own proxy objective and the parts were merely bolted together. And a new language meant a new phoneme inventory, a new hand-written lexicon, and new forced alignments before a single number improved.

Deep Speech 2 deletes the middle of the stack. The network reads a spectrogram and emits, at every output timestep, a probability distribution over the character alphabet plus one extra `blank` symbol.

**The trick that makes this possible is CTC**, and it solves a problem that had blocked the direct approach for years: nobody knows which audio frame corresponds to which letter, and labelling that by hand is infeasible. CTC sidesteps it. Define the loss as the total probability of *every* frame-by-frame symbol sequence that collapses down to the target transcript — merge repeated symbols, then drop the blanks — and sum over all of them by dynamic programming. Because the loss sums over all alignments, no alignment ever has to be supplied. **The label is just the raw transcript string.**

The stack itself is plain. The best English model puts three layers of 2D convolution, striding over time and frequency, under seven bidirectional recurrent layers and a fully connected output: roughly 100M parameters, using simple recurrent units, since GRUs stopped paying at that size. Two things made a network that deep trainable. Sequence-wise batch normalization computes each hidden unit's statistics over all items and all timesteps at once, rather than separately per timestep. SortaGrad orders the first epoch so that short utterances come first — easy examples before hard ones — which on the 9-layer model without batch norm moved dev WER from 11.96 to 10.83. Training ran on 8 or 16 GPUs with synchronous SGD and a hand-written all-reduce — the same exact-gradient discipline GPipe later carried across pipeline stages — over 11,940 hours of English and 9,400 hours of Mandarin, sustaining about 50 teraFLOP/s on 16 GPUs. That was a 7x speedup over the group's previous system, turning multi-week experiments into days.

Porting to Mandarin meant swapping the output layer to about 6,000 Chinese characters and the decoder to a character-level language model. **The architecture did not change at all**: no phoneme set, no lexicon, no tone model. On WSJ eval'92 the system reached 3.60 WER against 5.03 for Mechanical Turk transcribers, and 5.33 against 5.83 on LibriSpeech test-clean. Batch Dispatch grouped asynchronously arriving streams together, giving a 98th-percentile compute latency of 67 ms under 10 simultaneous streams. It earns its place for treating training throughput as a research variable rather than an ops detail, and for making on the model, three years early, the same trade GPipe made on parallelism: generic mechanism in place of hand-built structure.

> Iteration speed is an architectural choice: the 7x systems speedup is what let a generic model out-search a hand-engineered pipeline.

**The caveat:** human parity held on clean read speech and nowhere else. On LibriSpeech test-other the system scored 13.25 against 12.69 for humans, and that human baseline was crowdworkers rather than careful transcription. The model was also not the whole system: decoding still ran a beam search against a 5-gram language model, so "end-to-end" stopped short of the output. Bidirectional recurrence cannot stream, because it needs the end of the utterance before it can finish the beginning, so the deployed system swapped in forward-only layers. Attention and transducer models displaced the RNN-plus-CTC stack within a few years.
