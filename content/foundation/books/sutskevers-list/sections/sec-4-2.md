**Oriol Vinyals, Meire Fortunato, Navdeep Jaitly · 2015 · [arXiv:1506.03134](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.03134)**

A sequence-to-sequence model ends in a softmax over a vocabulary fixed when the model is built — fine for translation, where the target words are known in advance. It fails for problems whose outputs are *indices into the input*: sorting a list, returning the vertices of a planar convex hull in order, ordering cities into a tour. The number of output classes is the input length `n`, which changes from example to example; a model trained on 50 points cannot run on 5 or 500 without a new output layer. Bahdanau-style attention did not fix this: it used the soft weighting of 4.1 to blend encoder states into a context vector, then fed that into a fixed-width softmax.

The change stops one step short of existing machinery. An encoder RNN reads the input into hidden states `e_1 … e_n`. At decoder step `i` with state `d_i`, an additive score is computed for every input position, `u^i_j = v^T tanh(W1 e_j + W2 d_i)`, with `v`, `W1`, `W2` shared across positions. Standard attention normalises `u^i` and uses it as blending weights; Ptr-Net treats the normalised vector as the answer, `p(C_i | C_1…C_{i−1}, P) = softmax(u^i)` — a distribution of width `n`, growing and shrinking with the input. Decoding picks an input position; the embedding of that element becomes the next decoder input. There is no output projection matrix to size.

Trained by maximum likelihood on 1M input–solution pairs with a single-layer LSTM of 256 or 512 units, it beat the alternatives. On convex hulls with 50 points, trained and tested at that size, it produced the exactly correct vertex sequence 72.6% of the time, against 38.9% for an LSTM with input attention and 1.9% without. One model trained across 5–50 points scored 69.6% at `n = 50`, 50.3% at `n = 100` and 22.1% at `n = 200` — sizes it never saw. On planar TSP it matched the optimal tour at `n = 5` and came within 0.01 at `n = 10` (2.88 against 2.87). Trained at `n = 50` on tours from the weakest of three heuristics, it produced tours of 6.42 against that heuristic's own 6.46, beating its teacher.

The mechanism outlived the geometry. Reusing an attention distribution as an output distribution is the copy mechanism that later turned up in summarisation, dialogue and extractive question answering, where a span is named by pointing at start and end. It opened neural combinatorial optimisation, and 4.3 takes it as a decoder.

> Attention does not have to be a way of reading; the same distribution can be the answer.

**The caveat:** nothing enforces that a tour is a permutation. It will point at the same city twice, or skip one, so TSP decoding needed a beam search constrained to valid tours; the constraint lives in the decoder, not the network. Generalisation was narrower than the headline suggests: a model trained on 5–20 cities was near-optimal at `n = 25` and good at `n = 30`, but broke at 40, its tours averaging 5.91 against 5.82 for the crudest reference heuristic. And pointing can only emit what is already in the input, which is why later copy models mixed a pointer with a vocabulary softmax rather than replacing it.
