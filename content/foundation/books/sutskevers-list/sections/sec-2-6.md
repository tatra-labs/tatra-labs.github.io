**Alexander Rush · 2018 · [nlp.seas.harvard.edu](https://nlp.seas.harvard.edu/2018/04/03/attention.html)**

"Attention Is All You Need" specifies the model as equations and a block diagram. It fixes `N = 6` layers, `d_ff = 2048`, dropout 0.1, the widths listed above, and states that each sublayer output is `LayerNorm(x + Sublayer(x))`. What it does not give is the rank of the mask tensor, the point where a `(batch, len, 512)` activation becomes `(batch, 8, len, 64)`, how a padding mask and a causal mask combine into one object, or how Adam's state interacts with a warmup schedule. Reimplementations trained, produced plausible loss curves, and landed short. The post itself records an OpenNMT-py replication reaching 26.9 BLEU on WMT English–German against the 28.4 in the paper.

The post is the paper's text cut into pieces, each followed by the PyTorch that implements it: 400 lines of library code, runnable as a notebook. `attention()` is a scale, a mask, a softmax and two matmuls — divide `q·kᵀ` by `sqrt(d_k)`, `masked_fill(mask == 0, -1e9)`, softmax over the last dimension, dropout on the attention *weights* (which the paper leaves implicit), then multiply by `v`. Multi-head turns out to be a reshape rather than new math: one linear per q/k/v, then `.view(nbatches, -1, h, d_k).transpose(1, 2)` to fold heads into the batch dimension, the same `attention()` call, then transpose back and `.view(nbatches, -1, h*d_k)` into a final projection. Causal masking is `np.triu(np.ones((1, size, size)), k=1) == 0`; the leading `1` broadcasts across the batch, and the `Batch` class ands it with the padding mask so a single tensor carries both constraints.

The training scaffolding, which the paper compresses to a paragraph, gets the same treatment. `NoamOpt` wraps Adam with `lrate = d_model^-0.5 · min(step^-0.5, step · warmup^-1.5)` at `warmup = 4000`. `LabelSmoothing` is written as a KL divergence against a target distribution holding back 0.1 of its mass, not as a patched cross entropy. Batches are assembled by token count rather than sentence count — `BATCH_SIZE = 12000` tokens in the training script, against the 25000 source and target tokens the paper reports. A toy copy task trains in seconds, so each piece can be broken deliberately and the damage observed.

What changed is how the architecture got learned. The post became the standard on-ramp, and its naming — `EncoderDecoder`, `SublayerConnection`, `PositionwiseFeedForward`, `subsequent_mask` — propagated into other codebases. Rush published it at NLP-OSS 2018 as an argument about reproduction: a paper can carry enough detail to be replicated and still be hard to get right, so the implementation belongs inside the paper.

> An executable paper pins down what prose leaves free: tensor rank, where the reshape happens, and what the mask actually contains.

**What did not survive:** the infrastructure. The real-data path used torchtext's field and iterator API and a `MultiGPULossCompute` over `nn.DataParallel`; both were superseded, and the loss class was reported to fail on batches too small to scatter across every device. A 2022 refresh by Huang, Subramanian, Sum, Almubarak and Biderman rewrote that layer. One discrepancy sits in the code itself: `SublayerConnection` computes `x + dropout(sublayer(norm(x)))`, which is pre-norm, while the paper text beside it specifies post-norm. The code is closer to what trains stably. It is not what the paper says.
