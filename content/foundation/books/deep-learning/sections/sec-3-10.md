# 3.10 Useful Properties of Common Functions

Two functions and a list of identities. This is the most purely practical section in Chapter 3 — every
identity here exists to avoid a numerical failure that would otherwise happen in training.

## The two functions

The **logistic sigmoid**, which produces the $\phi$ of a Bernoulli distribution:

$$
\sigma(x) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp(-x)}
$$

It saturates: for large positive or negative $x$ the output flattens, and the function becomes
insensitive to its input.

The **softplus**, a smooth version of $\max(0, x)$, useful for producing a positive parameter such as a
standard deviation:

$$
\zeta(x) = \log\left(1 + \exp(x)\right)
$$

Its range is all of $(0, \infty)$, which is exactly the constraint a scale parameter needs.

## The identities

$$
\sigma(x) = \frac{\exp(x)}{\exp(x) + \exp(0)}
$$

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\sigma(x) = \sigma(x)(1 - \sigma(x))
$$

$$
1 - \sigma(x) = \sigma(-x)
$$

$$
\log \sigma(x) = -\zeta(-x)
$$

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\zeta(x) = \sigma(x)
$$

$$
\zeta(x) - \zeta(-x) = x
$$

The inverses are also named: $\sigma^{-1}(x) = \log\frac{x}{1 - x}$ is the **logit**, and
$\zeta^{-1}(x) = \log(\exp(x) - 1)$.

## Why they matter

The identity $\log \sigma(x) = -\zeta(-x)$ is the one that saves a training run. Computing $\sigma(x)$
and then taking its logarithm underflows to $-\infty$ for large negative $x$, because $\sigma(x)$ rounds
to zero in floating point. Evaluating $-\zeta(-x)$ instead is exact in the same regime. This is why
frameworks provide a fused `logsigmoid` and a `BCEWithLogitsLoss` that takes raw scores rather than
probabilities, and why passing an already-sigmoided value into a log-loss is a recurring bug.

The relation $\zeta(x) - \zeta(-x) = x$ is the softplus analogue of $1 - \sigma(x) = \sigma(-x)$, and the
derivative identity $\zeta' = \sigma$ is why the two functions always appear together.

## My take

The saturation property is the through-line of the book and it starts here. Because
$\sigma'(x) = \sigma(x)(1 - \sigma(x))$, the gradient through a saturated sigmoid is nearly zero, and
that single fact explains why deep sigmoid networks were hard to train, why the ReLU displaced the
sigmoid as a hidden unit in 6.3, why cross-entropy is paired with sigmoid outputs so that the log undoes
the exponential in 6.2, and why gates in an LSTM saturate deliberately in 10.10.

Read this section as the *mechanism*, then treat the next several chapters as its consequences.
