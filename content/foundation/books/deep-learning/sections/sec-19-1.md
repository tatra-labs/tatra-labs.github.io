# 19.1 Inference as Optimization

The single most important derivation in Part III. It converts an intractable summation into a
maximisation, and the object it produces is used by every variational method that follows.

## The setup

Inference means computing $p(h \mid v)$ — the posterior over latent variables given the observed ones —
or the log-likelihood $\log p(v; \theta)$, which requires marginalising $h$ out. Both are intractable for
the models of interest.

The trick is to introduce an arbitrary distribution $q(h \mid v)$ and construct a **lower bound** on the
log-likelihood:

$$
\mathcal{L}(v, \theta, q) = \log p(v; \theta) - D_{KL}\left( q(h \mid v) \parallel p(h \mid v; \theta) \right)
$$

Since the KL divergence is non-negative, $\mathcal{L}$ is at most $\log p(v)$, with equality exactly when
$q$ equals the true posterior. This is the **evidence lower bound**, or ELBO — also called the negative
variational free energy.

The bound can be rearranged into a form that is actually computable:

$$
\mathcal{L}(v, \theta, q) = \mathbb E_{h \sim q}\left[ \log p(h, v) \right] + H(q)
$$

the expected joint log-probability under $q$, plus the entropy of $q$. Nothing in this expression requires
the posterior — only the joint, which the model defines, and $q$, which is chosen.

## What this buys

Inference is now **optimisation**: search over distributions $q$ to make the bound as tight as possible.
The tighter the bound, the better $q$ approximates the posterior and the better $\mathcal{L}$ approximates
the log-likelihood.

The book notes that for some families of $q$ this can be done exactly and cheaply, and that when it
cannot, an incomplete optimisation still yields a valid lower bound — a partially-optimised $q$ gives a
looser bound rather than a wrong answer.

## My take

The rearranged form is the line to remember, because it is where the whole apparatus becomes practical.
The first version of the bound contains the posterior and is therefore uncomputable; the second contains
only the joint and the entropy and can be evaluated. They are algebraically identical, and the second is
what every implementation optimises.

The property that makes the framework robust is the one in the final paragraph, and it is easy to skip: a
poorly optimised $q$ gives a **valid but loose** bound rather than an invalid one. That is unusual. Most
approximations degrade into wrong answers; this one degrades into a weaker guarantee, which means a
variational method can be run with a limited budget and still report something meaningful.

Reading forward, this equation is the objective of a variational autoencoder, with $q$ produced by an
encoder network and the two terms becoming reconstruction and KL-to-prior. It is also the objective in the
ELBO-based derivation of diffusion models. And the reverse direction of the KL here — $q$ before $p$ — is
the mode-seeking direction from Section 3.13, which is the source of the systematic
underestimation of uncertainty that variational methods are known for. The bias is not incidental; it is
visible in the first equation of the section.
