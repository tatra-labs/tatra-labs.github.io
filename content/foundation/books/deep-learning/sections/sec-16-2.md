# 16.2 Using Graphs to Describe Model Structure

The formal apparatus of the chapter. Two graph languages, a third that unifies them, and the rules for
reading conditional independence off a picture.

## Directed models

A **directed acyclic graph** with one node per variable, and the distribution factorising into one
conditional per variable given its parents:

$$
p(x) = \prod_i p\left( x_i \mid Pa_{\mathcal{G}}(x_i) \right)
$$

Also called belief networks or Bayesian networks. Each factor is a proper conditional distribution, so
the product is normalised automatically. The cost of the table for one variable with $m$ parents each
taking $k$ values is $k^{m+1}$, which is manageable when $m$ is small even if $n$ is large.

Directed models are the right language when the structure has a natural **causal or generative order** —
when it is meaningful to say one variable was produced from another.

## Undirected models

An **undirected graph**, with the distribution factorising over **cliques**. Each clique
$\mathcal{C}$ gets a non-negative **factor** $\phi(\mathcal{C})$, and because the product of arbitrary
non-negative functions does not integrate to one, the result must be divided by a normalising constant:

$$
p(x) = \frac{1}{Z} \prod_i \phi^{(i)}\left( \mathcal{C}^{(i)} \right)
$$

$Z$ is the **partition function**, a sum over every configuration of every variable. Undirected models
are the right language when interactions are **symmetric** with no natural direction — the book's example
is which of your co-workers and housemates have caught a cold.

## Energy-based models

Requiring the factors to be strictly positive lets them be written as exponentials:

$$
\tilde p(x) = \exp\left( -E(x) \right)
$$

with $E$ the **energy function**. This is a **Boltzmann distribution**, and the reparameterisation is
convenient because any energy function gives a valid distribution, so a learning algorithm can adjust $E$
freely without worrying about maintaining positivity.

## Reading independence off the graph

For undirected models, **separation**: two sets of variables are independent given a third if every path
between them passes through the third. For directed models the rule is **d-separation**, which is more
intricate because of the explaining-away case from 3.7 — a path through a collider is *blocked* unless
the collider or one of its descendants is observed, which is the opposite of the usual behaviour.

The book is explicit that a graph shows only *some* of the independences that hold. A distribution may
have additional independences its graph does not display.

## Converting and factor graphs

Neither language subsumes the other. Converting a directed model to an undirected one requires
**moralisation** — marrying the parents of each node by connecting them — which adds edges and loses
independence information. Converting the other way requires the graph to be **chordal** or triangulated
first.

**Factor graphs** resolve an ambiguity in undirected notation: a clique of three nodes might carry one
factor over all three or three pairwise factors, and the graph looks identical. A factor graph adds
explicit nodes for the factors, making the parameterisation visible.

## My take

The most useful thing here is d-separation's collider rule, because it is the one that contradicts
intuition and the one that matters. Observing a common effect makes independent causes dependent, and
that single fact is the reason directed and undirected models are not translations of each other.

The energy-based reformulation is the entry with the most current relevance. Writing a distribution as
$\exp(-E(x))$ with a neural network computing $E$ is the modern energy-based model, and the reason such
models remained marginal is entirely $Z$ — the whole of Chapter 18 is about training them without
computing it. The connection worth carrying forward is that a diffusion model's score network estimates
$\nabla_x \log p(x)$, which is $-\nabla_x E(x)$, and the gradient of the energy does not involve $Z$
because $Z$ is constant in $x$. Score-based generative modelling is the energy-based programme finally
working, by never asking for the quantity that made it hard.
