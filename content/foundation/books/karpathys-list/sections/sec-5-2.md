**Wei, Wang, Schuurmans, Bosma, Ichter, Xia, Chi, Le & Zhou · 2022 · [arXiv:2201.11903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11903)**

The intervention is almost embarrassingly small. In the few-shot exemplars given to the model, do not show only the question and the answer — show the working. That is the whole method. The model, imitating the format, produces its own working before its own answer, and the answers get dramatically better.

The numbers made the case. PaLM `540B` with eight chain-of-thought exemplars reached `56.9%` on GSM8K, against `17.9%` with standard prompting, beating a GPT-3 that had been fine-tuned for the task and equipped with a verifier. No gradient was computed. Kojima et al. showed months later that even the exemplars were optional: appending "Let's think step by step" recovers much of the gain zero-shot. Wang et al. added self-consistency — sample many chains at temperature and take the majority answer — pushing GSM8K past `74%`.

**The mechanism is not really about reasoning, and reading it as a compute argument explains more.** A transformer performs a fixed amount of computation per token generated. Asked for an answer directly, it must produce the correct final token after a fixed depth of processing, no matter how much work the problem requires. Chain-of-thought lets the model **spend more forward passes on a harder question**, using its own output as a scratchpad that persists across them. Intermediate tokens are working memory. The reason it fails on small models — and the paper is clear that it hurts them — is that a model must already be capable of producing a *correct* intermediate step for the extra passes to compound rather than to accumulate error.

This is the first appearance of test-time compute as a usable axis, and it arrived disguised as a prompting trick, which is why it took the field another two years to treat it as the scaling story it was. [AlphaGo](/foundation/book/karpathys-list?section=sec-5-1) had the dial as an explicit search; here the dial is the length of the generation.

> The reasoning chain is not an explanation attached to the computation, it is the computation — extra tokens are extra forward passes, and the scratchpad is how they communicate.

**What did not survive:** chain-of-thought as a *prompting* technique. Nobody writes "let's think step by step" to a frontier model in 2026, because the behaviour has been trained in — first through instruction tuning on chain-annotated data, then through reinforcement learning against verifiable rewards, which produces chains far longer and stranger than any human would write as an exemplar. The paper's framing of the ability as **emergent above roughly `100B` parameters** also needs the correction in [the scaling entry](/foundation/book/karpathys-list?section=sec-3-3): much of the apparent discontinuity in curves like this is an artifact of exact-match scoring rather than a threshold in the model.

The finding that has aged worst, and that matters most, is the implicit assumption that the chain *is* the reasoning. Turpin et al. (2023) demonstrated that chains of thought can be systematically unfaithful: bias the few-shot examples so that the correct answer is always option (A), and models will follow that bias while producing a fluent chain that never mentions it and instead constructs a plausible-sounding justification for the biased answer. The stated reasoning was not the cause of the output. That result is the reason faithfulness is now a research area of its own, and it is the load-bearing caveat under every claim that a visible reasoning trace makes a system more interpretable — Lilian Weng's [Why We Think](/foundation/book/wengs-guide?section=sec-5-2) treats the current state of that problem directly.
