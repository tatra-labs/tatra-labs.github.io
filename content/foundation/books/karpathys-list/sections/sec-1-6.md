**Andrej Karpathy · 2024-2026 · [llm.c](https://github.com/karpathy/llm.c) · [nanochat](https://github.com/karpathy/nanochat) · [microgpt](https://karpathy.github.io/2026/02/12/microgpt/)**

Three repositories, two opposite directions, one decade-long argument. Karpathy describes microgpt as "the culmination of multiple projects (micrograd, makemore, nanogpt, etc.) and a decade-long obsession to simplify LLMs to their bare essentials," and the sequence only makes sense read as a whole.

**llm.c removes the framework.** GPT-2 training in plain C and CUDA, with the CPU reference implementation in roughly a thousand lines and no dependencies at all — every layer's forward and backward pass written out by hand, the optimiser included. The point is not speed, though the CUDA version is competitive. The point is that PyTorch is a convenience rather than a component. A transformer's backward pass is a page of arithmetic per layer, and once written down it compiles anywhere. Reproducing GPT-2 `124M` came in around ninety minutes on an eight-GPU node for roughly the price of a dinner, which converted "training a GPT-2" from a research result into an errand.

**nanochat goes the other way and removes the gaps.** Its subject is not the model but the pipeline: training a tokenizer, pretraining, midtraining, supervised fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, an inference engine with a cache for previously computed keys and values, and a chat interface — all in one legible repository of a few thousand lines. Every previous artifact in this chapter stops at a base model that merely completes text. nanochat is the first that ends with something you can talk to, which is exactly the step the [InstructGPT chapter](/foundation/book/karpathys-list?section=sec-4-1) explains and that no course had made runnable. The README's leaderboard currently reports a GPT-2-grade speedrun in about `1.65` hours on an `8xH100` node for roughly `$48`, or nearer `$15` on spot capacity.

**microgpt is the endpoint of the other axis.** Two hundred lines of pure Python, zero dependencies — not even NumPy — containing dataset loading, character-level tokenisation, a scalar autograd engine, a GPT-2-shaped transformer, the Adam optimiser, the training loop and sampling. It is [micrograd](/foundation/book/karpathys-list?section=sec-1-1) carried all the way through to a working language model, formatted to fit across three columns on a page. A companion gist walks the five stages from a bigram count table up to the full model as a series of diffs — the same teaching move as makemore's two bigram implementations, applied to the entire architecture.

**Reading the three together gives the shape of the claim.** There is a small, fixed algorithmic core — a few hundred lines, essentially stable for years — and around it an enormous amount of engineering that exists solely to make that core run fast enough, on enough data, to matter. Neither half is interesting on its own, and most explanations of language models silently pick one and pretend it is the whole story.

> Two hundred lines is the algorithm and eight thousand is the product; the distance between them is entirely data, schedule and kernels, and pretending otherwise in either direction is how people misjudge the field.

**What these artifacts do not demonstrate:** capability. microgpt is unusably slow by construction — a Python object per number is several orders of magnitude off anything practical — so it proves the algorithm is small and simultaneously proves that the algorithm is not what makes a model useful. nanochat's speedrun model is, by Karpathy's own description, roughly as coherent as a small child; the `$100` tier exists to make the pipeline reproducible, not to suggest the frontier is nearly within reach. The honest reading is that the gap between these repositories and a deployed assistant is four to five orders of magnitude of compute plus a data operation none of them contain, and that gap is the actual subject of chapters 3 and 4. Anyone who comes away from microgpt believing they have seen what GPT-5 is has read the code and missed the invoice.
