**Andrej Karpathy · 2024 · [Lecture 8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zduSFxRajkE) · [github.com/karpathy/minbpe](https://github.com/karpathy/minbpe)**

The lecture opens with a list of complaints about language models and gives the same answer to every one. Why can a model not spell, or reverse a string, or do arithmetic reliably? Why is it worse in Hindi than in English? Why did GPT-2 write bad Python? Why does a trailing space in your prompt degrade the output? **Tokenization.** The joke lands because the list is not rhetorical — each item has a mechanical explanation that the lecture then supplies.

Byte-pair encoding is simple enough to state in a sentence. Encode the text as UTF-8, which gives an alphabet of `256` byte values. Count adjacent pairs, find the most frequent, mint a new token for it, replace every occurrence, repeat until the vocabulary is the size you wanted. The tokenizer *is* the ordered list of merges. Encoding applies them in order; decoding is a lookup. minbpe implements this in a few hundred readable lines, and the training loop is a dictionary and an `argmax`.

What makes the lecture worth two hours is everything that is not the algorithm. **The regex split comes first.** GPT-2 does not run BPE on raw text; it first chops the text with a hand-written pattern so that merges can never cross certain boundaries — punctuation does not glue onto the preceding word, and runs of whitespace stay separate. GPT-4's pattern differs in ways that each encode a lesson: contractions are matched case-insensitively, whitespace is grouped so that Python indentation costs roughly half as many tokens, and **runs of digits are capped at three**, which is a direct attempt to fix arithmetic by making number tokenisation less arbitrary.

**Vocabulary size is a real trade-off, not a free parameter.** A larger vocabulary shortens sequences, which buys effective context and cheaper attention. It also means each token appears less often in training, so its embedding is fitted from less evidence, and it grows both the embedding matrix and the final softmax. GPT-2 chose `50257`; GPT-4's `cl100k_base` roughly doubles it.

The most instructive part is the failure gallery. **SolidGoldMagikarp** is a token that exists because the tokenizer was trained on a corpus containing a Reddit username thousands of times, while the model was trained on a corpus that had been cleaned of it. The token therefore has an embedding that received no gradient — it sits wherever initialisation put it — and prompting the model with it produces evasion, insults and non-sequiturs. It is a genuine unaligned input that no amount of safety training touches, because it was never in the training distribution at all. The trailing-whitespace problem is the same class of error in miniature: " world" is one token and " " followed by "world" is two, and the second is a sequence the model has essentially never seen.

> The tokenizer is a second model, trained on a different corpus, with its own vocabulary and its own failure modes, and every complaint about the language model's competence has to be checked against it first.

**What did not survive:** the resignation. The lecture ends by saying the ideal is to delete the tokenizer and feed bytes directly, and that nobody has shown this working at scale — which was accurate in early 2024 and is no longer the whole story. Meta's Byte Latent Transformer (December 2024) groups bytes into dynamically sized patches using a small entropy model, spending more compute where the next byte is unpredictable, and matched tokeniser-based training at comparable scale; H-Net and related hierarchical schemes followed. None of this has displaced BPE in production, so the practical advice stands. But the framing to carry forward is that tokenisation is a compression choice made before training and largely unexamined afterwards, not a permanent fact about language models. Several of the lecture's own complaints were then fixed by better tokenisers rather than by removing them: digit capping and per-digit splitting substantially repaired arithmetic, which is the strongest evidence for the lecture's thesis and against its pessimism.
