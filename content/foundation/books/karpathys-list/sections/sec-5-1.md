**Silver, Huang, Maddison, Guez, Sifre, van den Driessche, Schrittwieser, Antonoglou, Panneershelvam, Lanctot et al. · 2016 · [Nature 529, 484-489](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature16961)**

Karpathy uses this paper in the talk to name something a language model does not have. Kahneman's split is the frame: System 1 is fast, automatic, associative; System 2 is slow, effortful, deliberate. A transformer's forward pass is System 1 and only System 1. It spends **exactly the same computation on every token**, whether the next word is obvious or requires a proof. There is no dial. AlphaGo has the dial, and the paper is the clearest demonstration of what the dial buys.

The system has four networks and the division of labour is the point. A **supervised policy network** trained on `30M` expert positions predicts the move a strong human would play, reaching `57%` accuracy. A **fast rollout policy** does the same job far worse — `24.2%` — but in about two microseconds rather than three milliseconds, a ratio that makes it useful for a different purpose. A **reinforcement-learning policy network** improves the supervised one by self-play, winning around `80%` against its own teacher. A **value network** trained on self-play positions estimates who is winning from a position directly, using one position per game to avoid memorising correlated states.

Then search binds them. Monte Carlo tree search descends the tree choosing the action maximising `Q(s,a) + u(s,a)`, where `Q` is the accumulated value estimate and `u` is an exploration bonus proportional to the policy's prior probability and decaying with visit count — so the policy network says where to look first and the search decides whether it was right. A leaf is evaluated two ways and the results are mixed: the value network's estimate, and the outcome of a fast rollout played to the end with the cheap policy. Results are backed up the tree, and the whole loop repeats until the clock runs out.

**That last clause is the entire lesson.** The policy network alone plays strong amateur Go. The same network, given thinking time, beat Fan Hui `5-0` and Lee Sedol `4-1`, and won `99.8%` of games against other Go programs. Nothing was added to the network; time was converted into accuracy by an explicit search over futures, using the network as a prior rather than as an answer.

For an LLM in 2023 there was no equivalent. The model answers in one pass, at one cost, and if the first token is wrong the rest of the response is an elaboration of a mistake. Every entry that follows in this chapter is an attempt to build the dial.

> A policy gives you an intuition and a search converts wall-clock time into accuracy; a transformer's forward pass has the first and no mechanism at all for the second.

**What did not survive:** the transplant. The obvious move — bolt MCTS onto a language model — was tried extensively and mostly failed, for reasons that are structural rather than incidental. Go has a branching factor of about `250` and text has one the size of the vocabulary; Go has a terminal state and a definite winner, while an essay has neither; and the value network works because board positions are cheap to evaluate and self-play generates unlimited labelled outcomes, none of which holds for open-ended text. What actually worked, arriving in 2024 and 2025, was to **internalise the search into the policy**: train with reinforcement learning on long chains of reasoning against verifiable rewards until the model learns to backtrack, check and revise within a single linear generation. The dial exists now, and it is measured in output tokens rather than tree nodes. The insight this paper contributed — that search over a learned prior beats the prior alone, and that the exchange rate is time for quality — survived completely. The algorithm did not.
