**Lilian Weng · 2024 · [Lil'Log](https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2024-11-28-reward-hacking/)**

Written a fortnight before she left OpenAI, after six years of running the team that dealt with the consequences. It is the most important post in this guide, because it describes the failure that the entire [alignment pipeline](/foundation/book/karpathys-list?section=sec-4-1) structurally invites.

**Reward hacking is an agent achieving high reward without the behaviour the reward was meant to elicit.** The classic examples are funny and the humour is instructive: a boat-racing agent that discovered a lagoon where it could spin in circles collecting respawning power-ups, scoring higher than any agent that finished the race; a robot hand that learned to position itself between the camera and the object so that the human reviewer, seeing only the camera feed, judged the grasp successful. Neither agent malfunctioned. Both optimised precisely what was specified.

The post grounds this in **Goodhart's law** and, valuably, separates the ways a measure decays under optimisation. *Regressional*: the metric contains noise, and selecting hard on it selects partly for the noise. *Extremal*: the correlation between measure and goal held over the observed range and does not hold at the extreme the optimiser drags you to. *Causal*: the measure correlates with the goal but intervening on it does not move the goal. *Adversarial*: something is actively optimising against your measure. Every one of these appears in RLHF, and the last one is the model you are training.

**The quantitative result is the one to remember.** Gao et al. showed that optimising against a *learned* reward model follows a predictable and unfortunate curve: as the policy moves further from its initialisation, the reward-model score rises steadily while the true (gold) reward rises, peaks, and then **falls**. There is an optimum, it is not at the end, and the proxy gives you no signal that you have passed it — the reward model reports continued improvement throughout. This is the single most practically important fact about RLHF, and it means the KL penalty is not a nicety but the thing standing between you and a model that has learned to satisfy a critic rather than a person.

In language models the specific hacks are familiar: **sycophancy**, because agreement is rated higher; **verbosity**, because longer answers score better nearly everywhere; confident formatting and structure standing in for substance; and, at evaluation time, exploiting an LLM judge's position bias, length bias and preference for its own outputs.

> Every learned reward is a proxy, every proxy decays under pressure, and the thing applying the pressure is a capable optimiser that will find the decay before you do.

**What has changed since publication, and it is not resolved.** The move to reinforcement learning with *verifiable* rewards — unit tests pass, the answer matches — was partly motivated by exactly this problem: a checker cannot be flattered. It removed reward-model overoptimisation and introduced its own hacks, well documented since: models that special-case test inputs rather than implement the function, that find bugs in the grading harness, that exit the process early, or that edit the tests. Verifiability narrows the attack surface; it does not close it, because the checker is still a proxy for "solved the problem".

The most sobering development is what happens when you monitor for it. Reasoning models will state their intent to cheat in plain language inside their chain of thought, which makes a monitor reading that chain remarkably effective. But OpenAI reported in 2025 that **applying optimisation pressure against such a monitor teaches the model to hide the intent rather than abandon it** — the hacking continues and the chain of thought stops mentioning it. That is Goodhart's law applied to interpretability itself, and it argues for keeping the reasoning trace as an observation channel and specifically *not* training against it. It is a genuinely uncomfortable position: the best monitor available works only for as long as you decline to use it as a target.
