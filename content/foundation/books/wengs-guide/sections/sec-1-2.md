**Lilian Weng · 2018, revised through 2019 · [Lil'Log](https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2018-04-08-policy-gradient/)**

The most useful single document on this list. Roughly twenty algorithms — REINFORCE, actor-critic, A3C, A2C, DPG, DDPG, D4PG, MADDPG, TRPO, PPO, ACER, ACKTR, SAC, TD3, SVPG, IMPALA — derived in **one consistent notation**, with each presented as a fix for a specific defect in the one before it. The papers do not agree on symbols and mostly do not acknowledge each other's framing; this post makes them a single argument, and doing that is the expensive part nobody else did.

The spine is the **policy gradient theorem**, and its content is a deletion. You want to improve `J(theta)`, the expected return under a parameterised policy. Differentiating looks hopeless, because changing the policy changes which states you visit, and the state distribution `d(s)` depends on `theta` in a way you cannot compute without the environment's dynamics. The theorem says that term **vanishes**: the gradient is proportional to a sum over states of `d(s)` times a sum over actions of `Q(s,a) * grad pi(a|s)`, with no derivative of `d(s)` anywhere. You can sample states from the environment and ignore how the policy shaped that distribution. Without this, model-free policy optimisation does not exist.

Then the sequence of repairs, each addressing the previous method's failure.

**REINFORCE** plugs in the Monte Carlo return for `Q`. Unbiased, correct, and so high-variance it is often unusable. **Subtract a baseline** — any function of state — and the estimator stays unbiased while the variance drops sharply. **Actor-critic** makes the baseline a learned value function, which is where the variance-bias trade begins in earnest. **A3C** parallelises the data collection across workers to decorrelate samples; **A2C** observes that the asynchrony was incidental and batches synchronously instead.

**TRPO** addresses the step-size problem, which is peculiar to policy optimisation: a small change in parameters can produce a large change in behaviour, the agent then collects data under a much worse policy, and the run never recovers. TRPO constrains the KL divergence between successive policies, buying a monotonic improvement guarantee at the cost of a constrained optimisation problem with second-order machinery in it. **PPO** replaces the hard constraint with a **clipped surrogate objective**: form the probability ratio `r(theta) = pi_new(a|s)/pi_old(a|s)`, and clip it to `[1-eps, 1+eps]` so the objective flattens once the policy has moved far enough. First-order, a few lines of code, most of the benefit. That is why PPO won, and why it was sitting on the shelf when [InstructGPT](/foundation/book/karpathys-list?section=sec-4-1) needed an optimiser.

> Every algorithm after REINFORCE is buying variance reduction or step-size safety, and each purchase is paid for in bias, complexity, or both.

**What did not survive, and it is a satisfying reversal.** These algorithms were designed for continuous control — a robot arm, a walking gait — where episodes are long, rewards are sparse and delayed, and credit assignment across hundreds of timesteps is the entire difficulty. Transplanted into RLHF, PPO carries machinery for a problem that is not there. As [1.1](/foundation/book/wengs-guide?section=sec-1-1) notes, scoring one response is a bandit problem: no state transitions, no bootstrapping, nothing for generalised advantage estimation to estimate across.

The field spent several years discovering this. **GRPO** (2024) dropped the learned value network entirely, replacing the baseline with the mean reward of a group of sampled responses to the same prompt — a baseline computed from data rather than fitted, which is closer to REINFORCE-with-a-baseline than to actor-critic. Ahmadian et al.'s "Back to Basics" (2024) went further and showed plain REINFORCE-style estimators match or beat PPO for RLHF, because the variance PPO's critic was built to suppress is mostly absent in this setting. **The first algorithm in the post turned out to be the right one for its largest application**, after six years of increasingly elaborate alternatives. That is worth knowing before you spend a week tuning a critic that has nothing to do.
